## Part A: the word "sidelit"

| Question | Evidence | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| **In dictionaries?** | No entry for **sidelit** turned up for Merriam-Webster, Cambridge, Oxford, Collins or Wiktionary; they all have the noun **sidelight** ("light from the side"; also "incidental information") ([Oxford Learner's](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/sidelight), [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/dictionary/sidelight)). "Sidelit" appears as a gloss on a Russian learner site ("lit from the side", [skyeng](https://skyeng.ru/vocabulary/sidelight/)) and in photography glossaries ([matthughesphoto](https://www.matthughesphoto.com/s-photography/side-lit-photography-definition.html)). | A **niche, technical** adjective, not an everyday word. |
| **How common?** | Ngram not reachable. Web usage is photography ("sidelit portrait", exposure tips), signage (Steel Art's Sidelit™ signs) and LED lightboxes. | Rare in general English; known to photographers, sign makers, lighting people. |
| **What natives think of** | Light from the side; a portrait lit from one side; a lit sign. Nothing about watching, friends or "together". | No head start for our meaning; no bad meaning either. |
| **Non-native reading** | Turkish: "sayd-lit" if they know "side", otherwise "si-de-lit" (three syllables, like the Turkish place name *Side*). Spanish / Italian: "si-de-lit". German: "said-lit" (English loanword habit) or "zi-de-lit". Indonesian: "sai-lit" / "si-de-lit". | The spelling splits in two ways; many will say **"side-lit" only after hearing it once**. The Turkish resort **Side** may be the first association for Turkish users. |
| **Does it say "watching together / side by side"?** | Only through "side" (side by side, on my side) and "lit" (lit up, slang "exciting"). Nobody will guess "watch videos in sync" from the word alone. | It is an **evocative** name, not a descriptive one. |

**Does a tagline fix it?** Mostly yes, if it is always next to the name in the first months: "Sidelit — different
screens, same moment." / "Sidelit · watch together, side by side." The name gives the mood (warm light, beside you),
the tagline gives the job. This is the normal path for evocative names.

**How obscure-word brands made it work** (well-known histories, sources where found):
- **Slack**: a common word with an unrelated meaning; the founder later explained it as a backronym "Searchable Log
  of All Conversation and Knowledge" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slack_(software)),
  [Quartz](https://qz.com/794524/messaging-startup-slack-is-secretly-an-acronym-for-searchable-log-of-all-communication-and-knowledge)).
  It worked through a product people loved and invited teammates into, not through the word.
- **Discord**: a real word meaning disagreement, the opposite of what it sells; short, easy to say, ownable in its
  class (origin details not found here).
- **Twitch / Kick**: short everyday words, one syllable, easy to say in chat and on stream; the meaning comes from use.
- Common thread: **short (1–2 syllables), easy to say once heard, ownable in its class**, and the meaning is taught
  by the product and the invite link. Short names are easier to share in chats and on streams
  ([brandtune](https://www.brandtune.com/blog/gaming-startup-brand)).

**Verdict A: 🟡 workable.** "Sidelit" is little known, says nothing about watching, has two possible
pronunciations and a Turkish "Side" association; it is short, pleasant and has no bad meaning. With a fixed tagline
and the invite link doing the teaching it can work like Slack / Discord did. Biggest risks: people type "Sidelight",
and search results stay full of lighting for a long time (see `name-sidelit-check.md`).

## Part B: are simple names still free?

### Honest answer
- **Exact everyday words as .com: practically none.** All 2–4 letter .com names are registered; real words are
  registered or parked ([namebuddy](https://namebuddy.ai/guides/short-domain-name-ideas), vendor source). The current
  pattern is a short invented word of 5–7 letters ([BrandScout](https://brandscout-ashen.vercel.app/blog/tech-startup-naming-trends), vendor source).
- **In app stores, every friendly word is used by someone**, and in our own field (watch together) the plain words are
  crowded: Rave, Lobby, Watch Together, Hearo, WatchBuddy, CoWatch, CozyTwo, Minu…
- **A simple word can still be our trademark** in classes 9 / 38 / 41 if nobody uses it for the same services; a
  dictionary word is just harder to protect and to find in search.
- **Usual tricks:** keep the plain name, change only the address: `get` / `try` / `join` prefix (getmatinee.com),
  `-app` / `-hq` suffix, another ending (`.app`, `.tv`, `.watch`); two short words (Front Row, Watch Club); a small
  misspelling (Lyft, Flickr style); an invented word (Kosmi, Hulu are invented).

### Top 5 simple names
1. **Matinee**: a known English word with the right feeling (a film showing), and the same word in Turkish
   ("matine"); nothing in our field found. Address trick needed (getmatinee / matinee.app / matinee.tv).
2. **Cue**: three letters, "on cue" = together at the same moment; nothing in our field, but impossible to own in
   search and as .com; best as "Cue Watch" / cue.watch.
3. **Booth**: a small shared space; nothing in our field; plain but a little generic.
4. **Front Row**: the best seat in the cinema; caution for the old FrontRow camera brand (different goods).
5. **Chorus**: "all at once"; caution for small old apps with the name.

**Compared with Sidelit:** the simple words are easier to understand, but harder to own (search, domains, trademark
strength). Sidelit is easier to own and more distinctive, harder to understand without the tagline. **Matinee** is the
one simple word that both tells the story and works in Turkish.
